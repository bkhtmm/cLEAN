# Mathematical and formal provenance

Use the [shared review rules](_common.md). Preserve traceable credit while
moving, rewriting, or extracting material. Distinguish the source of mathematics
from the source of formal code and the agent's execution history.

Locate papers, books, notes, blueprints, prior formalizations, or vendored
upstream work that a development substantially follows. Check stated theorem
numbers and versions, and record changes to hypotheses/conclusions when known.
A standard cited result need not imply the library formalizes the entire paper.
Routine use of existing lemmas does not demand a citation for every dependency.

For copied or closely adapted formal code, preserve applicable notices and
existing source credit. Do not remove attribution merely because identifiers,
proof tactics, or file paths changed. For moved code, keep credit adjacent or in
the project's established source map so it remains discoverable. Unknown
provenance calls for investigation, not invented authorship or an unsupported
plagiarism allegation. Similar theorem order or notation can be evidence to
investigate; demonstrate the identifiable source before making a claim.

The user's codebase may have its own licensing and attribution conventions.
Flag an evident conflict rather than choosing new license terms during cleanup.
Do not inherit TauCeti's rule about placing roadmap credit only in a PR;
follow the target's established documentation and publication practice.

A finding gives the actual source, affected declarations/files, missing or lost
credit, and a concrete repair. Acceptance verifies source links/version,
retained notices where relevant, and accurate claim-to-source mapping. Missing
source access should be reported separately from confirmed missing attribution.
